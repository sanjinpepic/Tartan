# Design System: Tartan

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Photo Finish, Gone Native"**

Tartan is a phone and desktop app that feels like it ships with the device. Its surfaces are Material You tonal layers seeded from track tartan: a warm, faintly rosy paper ground, white cards with 28px corners, pill buttons, and pink-clay tonal containers. The chrome is iOS: a large title at the top of every view that scrolls away into a translucent blurred bar, inset grouped lists, segmented controls, and a workout sheet that grows out of the row you tapped. Phones get a bottom navigation bar with pill indicators. At 900px and up it becomes a navigation rail.

One object refuses to go native, and that is the point. The freshness strip is a blue-black timing board: white lane rules, condensed lane numerals, the time each source last crossed the line, and a red finish line at now. It is the only dark slab in a light app, and its condensed timing face is the one typographic import: it also carries the big measured figures elsewhere, so the race clock runs through the app. Everything around it is the platform's own UI face, deliberately: SF on Apple, Roboto on Android, Segoe UI on Windows, because the user's brief asked for a native iOS and Material You feel.

Data is the other voice. Charts are hand-drawn SVG with 2px lines, hairline grids and one shared crosshair. Fitness and pace are blue, fatigue and heart rate are tartan orange, and heart-rate zones step through a five-stop tartan ramp spaced evenly in OKLCH lightness. Each screen leads with a takeaway written as a sentence, not a number. Motion is one damped spring for things that move and one exponential ease-out for things that fade. Everything lands instantly under reduced motion.

The confirmed visual rejection is the fitness category default: activity rings, badge walls and neon stat cards on black. The app is unofficial and never imitates Suunto's branding.

**Key Characteristics:**
- Tonal surfaces seeded from tartan `#a8321f`. The warm ground, white cards and clay containers all carry the same hue.
- The timing-board strip in blue-black ink is the signature instrument and the one dark surface in light mode.
- The platform UI face for every word. Barlow Condensed (self-hosted) for timing-board numerals, the strip axis and the big measured figures (week distance, totals, workout stats, form).
- Pill controls, 28px cards and a corner scale that steps down by nesting: 28, 24, 20, 18, 14, 10.
- iOS large titles that collapse into a translucent bar, inset grouped lists, segmented controls, a bottom sheet on phone and a centered panel on desktop.
- A validated chart palette (blue, tartan orange, a five-step zone ramp) with light and dark variants.
- Status colors are reserved. They always come with an icon and a word.
- One spring, one ease-out, view transitions between tabs and into the workout sheet, all instant under reduced motion.

## Android 17: Material 3 Expressive (28 Oct 2026)

The owner asked for Android 17's design principles, with Orion Store as the Material You reference. This layer sits on top of everything below; where they disagree, this wins.

- **Material You.** In the Android app on Android 12+, the tonal tokens come from the wallpaper (`TartanThemePlugin`, `web/lib/theme.ts`). Android 14+ names every Material role; Android 12–13 use the tonal palettes at Material's tones. The mapping is primary, on-primary, the primary and secondary containers, inverse, page = `surface`, cards = `surface_container`, fields = `surface_container_highest` (dark: `_high`), tracks and pressed states = `surface_dim` (dark: `_highest`), outline = `outline_variant`, and ink = `on_surface` / `on_surface_variant` / `outline`. The colours are read again when you come back to the app. **Settings → More → Colours**: "Your wallpaper" (the default) or "Tartan".
- **What never follows the wallpaper:** the data palette (Pace Blue, Effort Orange), the zone ramp, the status colours, the timing board and the lane hues. A chart reads the same whatever the wallpaper (The Series Owns Its Hue Rule).
- **Tartan's own scheme** is now Material roles from the tartan seed (tonal spot surfaces, the brand's `#a8321f` primary). Light: page `#fff8f6`, cards `#fceae6`, fields `#f1dfdb`, tracks `#e8d6d3`. Dark: `#1a1110`, `#271d1c`, `#322826`, `#3d3230`.
- **Tonal, not shadowed.** Cards, group cards and empty cards have no shadow; they sit on the page by tone. Shadows stay for what floats: the composer, sheets, snackbars, the extended button, tooltips.
- **Lists are tiles.** `.rows.inset` rows are each a `surface-1` tile, 2px apart, with 24px corners at the group's ends and 6px between (Android's own Settings). There are no dividers. A pressed row rounds fully.
- **Choices are connected button groups.** Each segment is its own tonal button (`surface-2`): 8px where segments meet, 20px at the ends. The chosen one takes primary / on-primary and goes fully round. This covers `aria-checked`, `aria-selected` and `aria-current="page"`.
- **Shape morph on press.** Buttons go from pill to 12px corners (small ones 10px), icon buttons to 14px, action chips to 16px, all on the spring, and spring back.

## One design, two materials (28 Oct 2026)

Inspired by Stride (NikhilKain/stride, GPL-3.0: ideas only, nothing copied) and Orion Store: the bars float above the content as islands instead of spanning the screen. Tartan on Android and a future Tartan on iOS share this whole document: the same layout, type, shapes, spacing and motion. Only the material the floating bars are made of differs, set by `data-material` on `<html>` (`applyMaterial()` in `web/lib/theme.ts`).

| | Android, the web (`tonal`) | iOS (`glass`) |
|---|---|---|
| Floating bar fill | `surface_container_high`, solid | `surface_container_low` at 58% over a 22px blur, saturate 1.8 |
| Edge | none | a 1px white hairline (55% light, 14% dark) |
| Lift | Material elevation 2 (two soft shadows) | one wide soft shadow |

The tokens are `--float-bg`, `--float-filter`, `--float-edge` and `--float-shadow`. Anything that floats uses them, and nothing else does: cards stay tonal and flat on both.

- **Floating navigation pill.** On phones the destinations sit in a 64px pill, 12px above the gesture bar, as wide as the screen less 16px on each side and at most 420px. Each destination is a 48px rounded slot. The current one grows (flex 2.4 against 1), fills with `primary_container`, and shows its label beside the icon; the others are icon only. The width, the label and the fill all move on the spring, so switching tabs reads as one shape travelling. A badge cuts a ring of the bar's own colour around itself.
- **No top bar.** The page runs to the top. The connection chip (left) and the gear (right) float on their own in the float material; once the large title has scrolled away they gain the float shadow. The status bar keeps a strip of page colour that fades out over 10px, so text never runs under the clock. The collapsed title is gone: the large title is the only title.
- **Content scrolls under both** and ends above the pill (`--nav-h` is the pill, its gap and 8px). The composer, the ask bar, the snackbar and the route editor sit on top of `--nav-h`; with the keyboard open the pill goes.
- **Type: Google Sans Flex** (`web/fonts/`, SIL OFL, subset to Latin and Latin Extended, weight 300–900 with the ROND and optical size axes). Body text carries a little roundness (ROND 20); headings, hero figures and tile values are fully rounded (ROND 100). Numbers that need columns still use Barlow Condensed. On iOS the same file ships: the two versions look identical apart from the bars.
- **Tablets and desktop** keep the rail; the floating pill is the phone's.

### Native (Tartan 2, the `tartan-2` branch)
The same system in Jetpack Compose (`android/app/src/main/java/dev/freesuunto/app/shell/`), so a native screen beside a web one reads as one app: the same roles and values, Google Sans Flex with the same roundness, Barlow Condensed for figures.
- **Motion:** Material's expressive motion scheme. Spatial springs (which overshoot a little) for movement, size and shape; effects springs (no overshoot) for colour and opacity. Cards arrive one after another, rising 24dp, 50ms apart; things squash a little under the finger (to 96%, the bar's tab to 94%) and spring back. With animations off in Android's settings, the standard scheme.
- **Feel:** a short, deep click, never a buzz: Android's low-tick primitive, lighter (45%) for each tab slid past, fuller (90%) on tap or landing, played as touch feedback so the phone's vibration setting governs it.
- **The bar:** tap a tab, or press anywhere and slide: the light follows the finger with a click per tab, the bar grows 3% while held, and letting go chooses. Dragging up off the bar cancels.
- **The week:** a wavy ring (Material's circular wavy progress) filling toward the plan's distance, your target or your usual week, green and flat once reached; the number counts up with it.

## Colors

The palette is a Material You tonal scheme seeded from track tartan, plus a timing-board ink set used only inside the strip, a validated data palette, and a reserved status set. All tokens live on `:root` in `web/styles.css` and are redefined under `prefers-color-scheme: dark`. The frontmatter records each dark value as `<token>-dark`.

### Named Rules
**The Tonal Seed Rule.** Every surface, container and outline is a tone of the tartan seed. A new neutral must carry the same warm hue. No cool grays outside the timing board.

**The Board Is the Only Dark Slab Rule.** Board Ink appears only behind the freshness strip. In light mode no other card, band or panel is dark.

**The Reserved Status Rule.** Good, Watch and Error are status only, and every use pairs the color with an icon and a word ("Good", "Watch", "Note"; a check, a warning triangle). Status color never decorates, and it never carries meaning alone.

**The Series Owns Its Hue Rule.** Pace Blue means fitness and pace, Effort Orange means fatigue and heart rate, on every chart and legend. Text next to a series never wears the series color. The legend key carries it instead.

## Typography

**UI Font:** the platform system face (system-ui, -apple-system, BlinkMacSystemFont, Segoe UI Variable, Segoe UI, Roboto, Helvetica Neue, Arial), so it renders as SF on Apple, Roboto on Android and Segoe UI on Windows.
**Timing Font:** Barlow Condensed 500 and 600, self-hosted from `web/fonts/` (falls back to Arial Narrow, then the UI face).
**Code Font:** the platform monospace (ui-monospace, SF Mono, SFMono-Regular, Menlo, Consolas, Roboto Mono).

**Character:** The platform face is a deliberate choice that follows the user's pinned brief for a native iOS and Material You feel. Hierarchy comes from heavy weights (700 to 760) and tight negative tracking at the top, the way iOS large titles do it. The timing face is the race clock beside it: on the board, and on every big measured figure.

- **Large title** (750, 2.125rem, 2.5rem at 900px and up, line-height 1.12, -0.025em): one per view ("Today", "Activities"). It scrolls under the top bar, and its text then appears there at 650 / 1.0625rem.
- **Headline** (740, 1.5rem, -0.02em): the workout sheet title. The coach's empty state uses 1.625rem.
- **Section** (700, 1.25rem, -0.015em): headings above groups of cards, such as "Takeaways", and the session, race and weekly card heads on Today (720).
- **Hero** (760, 2rem, -0.02em): the readiness word on Today (1.625rem on Body).
- **Title** (650, 1.1875rem, -0.012em): card headings.
- **Subtitle** (620, 1.0625rem): h3, group heads at 680 / 0.9375rem, takeaway titles at 680.
- **Stat** (720, 1.375rem, -0.02em, tabular): sheet stat cells and the form pill. KPIs use 680 / 1.0625rem.

- **Code** (monospace 0.8125rem / 1.55): paths, snippets, the log and MCP tool names. Inline code is 0.86em on Warm Mist.

### Named Rules
**The Timing Face Is for Measured Figures Rule.** Barlow Condensed is the race clock. It sets lane times, lane numerals and the strip axis on the board, and the measured figures that answer "how far, how fast, how much" elsewhere: the week's distance, the Trends totals, the workout stat tiles and the form value. It never sets words, labels, units, chart ticks or body copy; units beside a timing figure stay in the UI face.

**The Tabular Numbers Rule.** Every number that changes or sits in a column uses tabular figures: times, figures, stats, KPIs, table cells, chart ticks and tooltips.

**The Weight Not Case Rule.** Hierarchy comes from size, weight (400 to 760) and tracking. There are no uppercase labels and no tracked-out small caps.

## Elevation & Depth

The system is tonal first, with soft warm shadows. Depth comes from the ground-to-white step between Infield Paper and Lane White, reinforced by three shadow levels tinted with tartan brown in light mode. In dark mode shadows turn to black at higher opacity, and the strip gains a 1px Hairline outline because a shadow cannot lift dark off dark. Translucency is the second depth tool: the top bar and phone navigation bar are blurred, saturated glass over content.

### Shadow Vocabulary
- **Resting** (`--shadow-1`: `0 1px 2px rgba(74, 28, 16, 0.05), 0 2px 8px rgba(74, 28, 16, 0.04)`; dark `0 1px 2px rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.3)`): cards, inset lists, tiles, takeaway cards, assistant bubbles, the selected segment, filled-button hover.
- **Raised** (`--shadow-2`: `0 2px 6px rgba(74, 28, 16, 0.08), 0 10px 28px rgba(74, 28, 16, 0.10)`; dark `0 2px 6px rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.35), 0 10px 28px rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.3)`): the freshness strip, the extended Sync button, the composer, chart tooltips, hovered takeaway cards.
- **Overlay** (`--shadow-3`: `0 8px 24px rgba(40, 12, 6, 0.16), 0 24px 64px rgba(40, 12, 6, 0.18)`; dark `0 8px 24px rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.45), 0 24px 64px rgba(0, 0, 0, 0.5)`): the sheet, the snackbar, the hovered Sync button.
- **Scrim** (`rgba(20, 8, 6, 0.38)`): behind the sheet.
- **Runner glow** (`-12px 0 16px -6px` in the lane hue): the leading edge of a syncing lane. Light, not depth.

### Named Rules
**The Three Levels Rule.** Resting for content, Raised for the strip and floating controls, Overlay for sheets and snackbars. A fourth level is not added. Hover moves an element up one level, never two.

## Shapes

Corners are generous and they nest: an outer container's radius is always larger than what sits inside it. **Panel** (32px) is the desktop sheet. **Card** (28px) is cards, the strip, the composer and the top of the phone sheet. **Tile** (24px) is tiles, takeaway cards, inset lists, flat cards, the route card and the strip below 700px. **Inner** (20px) is rows inside inset lists and job cards. **Row** (18px) is list rows, stat cells and the extended button. **Field** (14px) is inputs, row icons and table frames. **Chip** (10px) is chips and the zone bar. **Inline** (6px) is inline code and the focus ring. Nothing sits between these steps (no 12px or 16px corners): small thumbnails and icon tiles take Field, containers inside cards take Row. **Pill** is every button, icon button, segment, search field, navigation indicator, status badge and connection pill: the radius is always half the height.

Chat bubbles keep one tight 6px corner toward their speaker (`22px 22px 6px 22px` for the user, mirrored for the assistant). Column bars have 4px rounded data ends. Timing-board marks are 3px wide with 2px rounded tops, and the finish line is a 3px bar. The connection lamp and lane dots are the only circles.

The app mark is three lane lines at 55% opacity crossed by a heavier finish line, white on a tartan rounded square (rx 9 of 32 in the rail, rx 7 of 32 in the favicon).

### Motion
- **Curves:** `--spring` is a damped spring with about 5% overshoot (a `linear()` curve, cubic-bezier fallback) for anything that moves: presses, pills, the snackbar, chart columns, timing ticks and marks. `--spring-soft` (about 2% overshoot) carries larger travel: the sheet, view transitions, reveals and messages. `--ease-out` (`cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)`, exponential) is for fades, colors and line draws. The sidecar holds the full values.
- **Durations:** presses 80ms; state layers 140 to 220ms; spring moves 260 to 520ms; reveals 600ms fade and 760ms rise, staggered by 70ms; chart lines draw in once over 1.4s and the route over 1.8s; columns grow with an 18ms stagger; count-ups run 700ms on an exponential ease-out.
- **View transitions:** between tabs the old view fades out in 140ms and the new one rises 14px on the soft spring; the navigation and top bar stay still. Opening a workout morphs the tapped row into the sheet over 520ms.
- **Reveals happen once.** Cards rise in on a view's first paint only. After that, data changes under a still frame with `swap()` (`web/lib/motion.ts`): the old content leaves in 130ms, the new arrives on the ease-out in 420ms, sliding from the side you moved toward (week arrows, range and filter segments, This week / Whole plan, Settings groups) or crossfading in place. Numbers count from their previous value, not from zero. A local update (a session ticked off) just changes.
- **The sheet has one entrance.** It morphs out of the tapped row at its full height; when the morph runs, the CSS slide-up is off (`.morphed`), and a workout that isn't ready within 220ms shows the sheet's own outline (head, route, stat grid), which dissolves into the content without the sheet moving. Focus goes to the sheet, never to its first chart.
- **First run is the one authored moment.** The welcome is a small timing board: Watch, Strava and Coach run to the Finish Red line one after another (1.5s each, 260ms apart), each numeral taking its lane hue as it crosses, and the words rise as the first lane finishes. The ground is opaque from the first frame. Steps slide sideways; the progress bar fills on the soft spring.
- **App, not page.** The bars are fixed and `#main` is the only scroller between them, so nothing the content does can move them. The content has its own rubber band at both ends (`web/lib/rubber.ts`, the iOS resistance curve, a 520ms ease-out back); the page and the WebView never stretch. No text selection or long-press callouts outside fields and copyable code, no web scrollbars in the app, a light haptic tick on tabs, segments and answer chips and a firmer one when something lands.
- **Reduced motion:** every animation, transition and view transition completes in 1ms with no delay, reveals start visible, and the lane runner is hidden. Only the busy spinners keep turning, slowed to 1.6s, because they report state.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:
- **Do** seed every new surface, container and outline from the tartan tonal scheme, and give it a dark-mode value in the same block.
- **Do** keep the timing board as the one dark surface, with white lane rules, condensed lane numerals and the Finish Red line at now.
- **Do** use pill buttons (radius half the height, 44px minimum) and nest corners from the scale: 32, 28, 24, 20, 18, 14, 10, 6.
- **Do** open each view with a large title; on phones it is the only title, and it scrolls away under the floating chips.
- **Do** group lists as inset rows with inset hairline dividers, and use a segmented control for a single choice among two to five options.
- **Do** chart fitness and pace in Pace Blue, fatigue and heart rate in Effort Orange, zones in the five-step ramp, and ship a table twin with every chart.
- **Do** pair every status color with an icon and a word.
- **Do** move things on the spring, fade things on the ease-out, and let everything land instantly under reduced motion.
- **Do** ship Google Sans Flex and Barlow Condensed from `web/fonts/` so the Android shell renders offline.
- **Do** make anything that floats out of the `--float-*` tokens, so iOS can swap tonal for glass in one place.

### Don't:
- **Don't** set words, labels, units or chart ticks in Barlow Condensed.
- **Don't** add a second dark slab, a neon stat card on black, activity rings or a badge wall.
- **Don't** use Good, Watch or Error as decoration, or let a status color carry meaning without an icon and a word.
- **Don't** use Finish Red or Board Amber outside the strip.
- **Don't** give text the color of the series it describes. The legend key carries the hue.
- **Don't** add a fourth shadow level or a hard offset shadow.
- **Don't** use square or barely rounded buttons, or a corner radius that is not on the scale.
- **Don't** add uppercase or letter-spaced eyebrow labels above headings.
- **Don't** use glyph or emoji icons, hosted web fonts or icon fonts.
- **Don't** imitate Suunto's branding in color, logo or naming.
